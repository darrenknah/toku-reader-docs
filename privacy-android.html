Privacy Policy — Toku Reader for Android

Last updated: August 4, 2026

Toku Reader ("the App") is a Japanese and Chinese reading assistant developed by Darren Nah. The App has no account, collects nothing about you, and sends nothing you read, listen to, or save to the developer. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, and no crash-reporting SDK in the App.

This policy covers the Android app (package com.darren.tokureader). The iOS app is a separate product with a separate policy; where the two differ, this page governs Android.

What the developer collects

Nothing. There is no sign-up, no login, no user identifier, no device identifier generated by the App, and no server operated by the developer that the App talks to. The App does not phone home — not on launch, not on use, not ever.

Everything happens on your device

The work the App does for you is done on your phone:

  • Reading — text you paste, share, import or scan is analysed on-device: word boundaries, readings (furigana / pinyin), and dictionary meanings. It is stored only on your phone.
  • Dictionaries — the Japanese and Chinese dictionaries are bundled inside the App. Lookups make no network request.
  • Saved words and review — your saved words, review history and scheduling live in a database on your phone.
  • Transcribing podcasts — when a show publishes no transcript, the App writes one on your phone using a speech model that runs locally. The audio is not uploaded to anyone for this.
  • Reading photos (OCR) — the Japanese and Chinese text recognition models are bundled inside the App. Photos are read on-device and are never uploaded or stored by the App.
  • Text-to-speech — spoken readings use the text-to-speech engine already on your Android device.
  • Crash reports — if the App closes unexpectedly, it writes the technical details to a file on your phone and offers to share it the next time you open the App. Nothing is sent unless you tap Send and choose where it goes. You can dismiss it and the file is deleted.

When the App uses the network

The App connects to the internet only to do a thing you asked for. In each case the request goes to that third party directly — it does not pass through, and is not logged by, any server of the developer's.

  • Podcasts — to show the podcast directory, fetch a show's RSS feed, and download or stream an episode, the App contacts the podcast's own host. Those hosts see a normal media request, as any podcast app would produce.
  • Web Reader — the in-app browser loads the pages you navigate to, using Android System WebView (the same engine Chrome uses). The App does not collect, log or transmit your browsing history, the addresses you visit, or page contents. Sites you visit see you as they would in any browser, and their own privacy policies and cookies apply to them.
  • YouTube links — if you paste a YouTube link to read along with its captions, the App requests that video's captions from YouTube.
  • On-device model downloads — two optional features download a model from Google the first time you use them, and then work offline forever after: handwriting input in the Dictionary tab (about 20 MB per language) and Show English gloss translation. If you never use those features, nothing is downloaded. The recognition and translation themselves run on your device.
  • Google Play Billing — if the App ever offers a purchase, it is handled by Google Play. The developer never sees your payment details.

Optional: connecting WaniKani

The App can optionally connect to WaniKani, a third-party Japanese study service, so your WaniKani progress appears alongside your saved words. This is off unless you set it up, and the App is fully usable without it.

  • You generate a Personal Access Token on wanikani.com and paste it into the App. It is stored in encrypted, hardware-backed storage on your device (Android Keystore).
  • The token is sent only to api.wanikani.com, over HTTPS, to read your WaniKani data. It is never sent to the developer.
  • What WaniKani does with that request is governed by WaniKani's own privacy policy.
  • You can disconnect at any time, which deletes the stored token.

Permissions the App asks for, and why

  • Internet / network state — to fetch podcasts and web pages, and to tell "you're offline" apart from "that site is broken" when a page fails to load.
  • Microphone — used only by shadowing practice, where you record yourself repeating a sentence to compare with the speaker. The App does not touch the microphone until you tap Record yourself; opening the practice screen does not activate it. Recordings stay in the App's private cache on your phone, are not uploaded anywhere, and are not used for speech recognition by any server.
  • Foreground service (media playback) — so podcast audio keeps playing when the screen turns off, with the usual notification controls.

The App does not request access to your contacts, your location, your call logs, your messages, or your photo library as a whole. When you pick a photo or a video, Android's own picker hands the App only the one file you chose.

Third-party components

The App uses Google ML Kit for on-device text recognition, handwriting recognition and gloss translation. Recognition and translation run on your device. Note that ML Kit is a Google SDK and, as its own default behaviour, may generate a pseudonymous instance identifier and send basic SDK diagnostics to Google the first time it is used. That is Google's behaviour, not something the App requests or receives. See Google ML Kit's terms and Google's Privacy Policy.

To avoid ML Kit entirely, do not use photo/OCR reading, the handwriting canvas, or Show English. Every other feature — reading, dictionary search, podcasts, transcripts, saved words and review — works without it.

Children

The App is a language-learning tool for a general audience. It is not directed to children, and it does not knowingly collect anything from anyone — including children, since it collects nothing from anyone at all. Note that the App includes a web browser, so a young user could reach general web content through it; supervision is advised for children.

Data retention and deletion

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct, export or delete from a server. Everything the App knows about you is on your phone. Uninstalling the App, or using Android's Clear storage, removes it. Downloaded episodes, transcripts and speech models can also be cleared individually in Settings › Playback › Storage.

Your rights

Privacy laws including the GDPR (EU/EEA), the UK GDPR, the APPI (Japan) and the CCPA/CPRA (California) give you rights over personal data a company holds about you — access, correction, deletion, portability, and objection to processing. The developer holds no personal data about Android users, so there is nothing to act on; if you believe otherwise, use the contact address below and it will be investigated.

The App does not sell or share personal information, and does not use your data for advertising or tracking — because it does not have any.

Changes to this policy

If the App ever begins collecting anything, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the "last updated" date above will change.
